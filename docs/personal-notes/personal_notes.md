# Personal training notes

Your own notes, separated from the Sundog class materials. Skills marked *(yours only)* don't appear in the Sundog library at all; the others are skills Sundog also teaches, with what *your* notes added.

## Cookie fingers

A signal that means 'come to this hand, there's food'. The basis for luring and for many handling moves.

## Hand targeting ('Touch')

Nose-to-palm touch. A portable focus tool, a distraction from trouble, and the first step toward contact training.

## It's Your Choice (the 'Mugging' game)

Self-control taught through body language only: the treats are available when the dog stops trying to take them.

## Leave it

Don't touch that: food on the floor, a sock, a toy. A safety skill (e.g. broken glass with food on the kitchen floor), and later used in agility when a toy on the ground is the reward.

From your notes:
- Test: throw a good treat nearby, say 'leave it', and the dog comes to you instead.
- Test: off leash, walking close by the toy.

## Take it / Get it / OK (permission) & 'This and That'

The dog waits for permission instead of lunging at dropped things. Builds patience and impulse control.

## Waiting at doors

Safety skill: the dog never rushes through an opening door, whichever side you're on.

> Your notes say 'Description…'. The steps above are taken from the class's crate-door progression (F03) and 'Your In Your Out with crates, gates, doors' (F08). The class notes don't have a separate door lesson.

## Name Game (response to name)

The dog snaps its head to you when it hears its name. This is the first 'recall' a puppy learns.

## Formal recall: 'Here' / 'Come'

Emergency-level cue: come right to me, fast. Possibly lifesaving.

> Your notes say 'Describe…'. Steps above are taken from F03/F05/PK4.

## Mat work ('Go to bed' / 'Place')

Going to the mat means lie down and relax until released.

From your notes:
- Test commitment with 'Boundary Games' (free PDF from Absolute Dogs).

## Off  *(yours only)*

Get off the bed, chair, or whatever.

Cues: 'Off'

1. Lure the dog onto something (not too high).
2. Say 'off' and throw a kibble/treat on the floor.
3. Repeat; they catch on fast.

- Pairs nicely with 'Feet' as an active attention game.

## Luring onto objects & new surfaces

Confidence on different textures and objects; the foundation for perch work and contacts.

## 'Feet' (front feet on a target)  *(yours only)*

Point to something and the dog puts its front feet on it.

Cues: 'Feet'

1. Lure front feet onto an object while saying 'feet'.
2. Fade the lure to a point.

- Pair with 'Off' as an active attention game.

## Perch work: rear-end awareness (pivots)

Teach the dog it has a back end it can move independently while the front feet stay put, and to move away from handler pressure.

## Flip away (turning away from you)

The dog reads a turn away from you. It's the foundation for lap turns.

## 'Around' (go around a cone)  *(yours only)*

Send the dog around an object.

Cues: 'Around'

1. Dog at your side. Say 'around' and extend your hand toward the cone.
2. Lure around it the first few times.
3. Practise the other side.
4. Vary the object, where you stand, and the dog's distance from the object.

- Overlaps with the class's lap turn and wrap; see those for handling details.

## Shake / High five  *(yours only)*

Classic paw shake; once mastered, change it to a high five.

Cues: 'Shake', 'High five'

## Between  *(yours only)*

Go between my legs and sit. A safe spot for small dogs where you can protect them.

Cues: 'Between'

1. Treats in both hands.
2. Say 'between' and lure the dog toward you, but keep the lure hand going past and behind your leg.
3. Show the other hand to bring the dog forward through your legs; ask for the sit.


## Leave it & Take it: your methods

- Drop a kibble far enough away that the dog can't easily get it, say 'leave it', and reward with a better treat from you. Slowly decrease the distance. Test: throw a good treat nearby, say 'leave it', and the dog comes to you instead.
- On leash, walk past a good toy (or treat), say 'leave it' and keep going with gentle leash pressure. Test: off leash, walking close by.
- Take it: drop a kibble and cover it with your hand; don't lift until they back away, then say 'take it'. Slowly extend the wait. Works with toys too.

## Standing on weird things & rear-end awareness: your additions

- Upside-down dog bowls work great; squishy exercise mats help balance. Lure front feet on with 'feet', later back feet only.
- For pivots, a squeeze tube of soft dog food or peanut butter (xylitol-free) gives the dog something to lick while you step around.
