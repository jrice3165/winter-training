## Training principles

<a id="training-session-basics"></a>
### Training session basics
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation*

How every session should be set up so the dog learns quickly and enjoys it.

**How to**
1. Keep sessions short; spread games across the day or week instead of doing everything at once (e.g. 1 minute of Name Game before a meal).
2. Each exercise uses about 5–8 treats/rewards, then that session is done.
3. Prepare first: treats cut to size, toys, target plates, etc. all ready before you start.
4. Young or easily distracted dogs: train somewhere quiet, away from other pets and kids. Older dogs with skills: add mild distractions (yard, park).
5. Foundation class: do all exercises on leash so the dog stays connected to you.
6. Train when you're in a good mood. Be positive and fun; you're a team.
7. Start each session with warm-ups (see Warm-ups).

**Tips**
- Immediate rewards = faster learning, fewer repetitions, longer retention, faster performance. Late rewards = slow or no learning.
- You have about 1 second for a reward or consequence to make sense to the dog (PK3).

<sub>Sources: F02, PK1, F03, F04, F05</sub>

<a id="positive-reinforcement"></a>
### Positive reinforcement & finding what your dog values
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation*

Training is play: find what your dog values most and use games to show they get it by playing with you and making the right choices.

**How to**
1. List what your dog will work for: food, toys, praise, play.
2. Reward immediately when the dog makes the right choice.
3. Avoid confrontation and verbal corrections. Control access to rewards instead (e.g. closing your hand, closing the crate door).

**Tips**
- Dogs always do what's rewarding. The goal is teaching that rewards come through doing things with/for you, not by helping themselves (PK3).
- Motivators: food drive, toy drive, prey drive. Each has pros and cons. Aim for a balance so the dog is both handler-focused and obstacle-focused, and can work at a distance (F03).

<sub>Sources: F02, PK1, F03, PK3</sub>

<a id="verbal-cues-system"></a>
### Using verbal cues
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation, puppy*

Rules for choosing and using cue words so they mean something the first time.

**How to**
1. Pick short words that make sense to you and use them consistently.
2. Say a cue once (maybe twice if the dog is really struggling), then help with a lure rather than repeating it.
3. Build value for each word with great rewards (food/toys).
4. Choose one release word and stick with it. Short words are easier to say while running.

**Tips**
- Dogs respond to body language first, words second. Body language usually overrides verbals. 'Less is more': save verbals for things that matter.
- See the Cue Glossary for every word used in the classes.

**More:** [Whisper training (video)](https://vimeo.com/651673085/91eda2810a)

<sub>Sources: F02, F03, F08, F14, PK1</sub>

<a id="warmups"></a>
### Warm-ups
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation*

Get the dog focused, engaged and in the right arousal level before work.

**How to**
1. Start every session with something fun: stretches, known tricks, basic obedience, tug, Push Away and Run.
2. If the dog is too high to think: use calm but fun warm-ups or calming games.
3. If the dog is flat: rev them up with exciting play, then ask for focused work.

**Leads to:** [Push Away and Run](#push-away-and-run)

## Markers, clicker & shaping

<a id="yes-marker"></a>
### Teaching 'Yes' (marker word)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation · Cues: 'Yes', 'Good'*

'Yes' means the exercise is over and a reward is coming right now.

**How to**
1. Say 'Yes', feed a treat. Repeat until the treats are gone.

**Tips**
- 'Good' is different: it means keep going, you're doing it right (continue the exercise).
- Words can carry emotion (excited or disappointed tone); a clicker always sounds the same (F06).

**Leads to:** [Charging the clicker](#charge-clicker)

<sub>Sources: PK1, PK3, F03</sub>

<a id="charge-clicker"></a>
### Charging the clicker
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation · Cues: '(click)'*

Teach the dog that the click always means a treat is coming.

**You'll need:** clicker; small high-value treats

**How to**
1. Have a handful of really good treats cut small.
2. Click once, then feed. The treat follows the click, not at the same time. Click > treat, click > treat.
3. Don't point the clicker at the dog like a remote; hold it at your side or behind your back.
4. Repeat standing, sitting, moving, and in different places.
5. A few times a day, 6–10 treats each. Done when the dog visibly anticipates the treat at the click.

**Leads to:** [Shaping & capturing](#shaping)

<sub>Sources: F05, F06</sub>

<a id="shaping"></a>
### Shaping & capturing
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation*

Build behaviours from scratch, with no physical control or corrections, by marking small steps toward a goal.

**You'll need:** clicker (or 'Yes'); treats; novel objects: pizza box, book, mat, pot, box, perch

**Before this:** [Charging the clicker](#charge-clicker)

**How to**
1. Capturing: wait for the dog to do the whole behaviour (e.g. sit), mark and reward it, then add a cue once it happens often.
2. Shaping: mark and reward successive small steps toward the final behaviour.
3. Novel-object starter (F07): put an object down, stare at it. No words, no luring. Click & feed for looking at it, moving toward it, nose-touching it, a foot on it.
4. Raise criteria gradually. At each new level reward every time for a handful of reps, then reward randomly, then raise criteria again.
5. Add the cue only once the final behaviour is reliable; then reward only cued reps.

**Progressions**
- `F07` Free interaction with novel objects (look, approach, nose, foot).
- `F07` Specific behaviours: one foot on the board, four feet on the board, nose touch.
- `F11` Clicker challenge: all 4 feet in a box, OR front feet on 2 separate objects (e.g. 2 small books). Also review nose touch on an object.
- `F12` Rotate challenges between sessions (nose touch one session, box work the next). Bring items to class to demo.

**Example: Ring a bell (to ask to go out)** (`F07`)
1. Hang the bell where you want it (e.g. by the door).
2. Start near the bell; wait and watch.
3. Click & feed for looking at the bell; reward a few looks.
4. Raise criteria: one step toward the bell.
5. Keep raising: closer and closer, then only touching it.
6. Then only reward loud rings, not feeble ones.
7. Add the cue; if it's for potty, use going outside as the reward.

**Tips**
- Shaping needs good observation and timing; it's the more advanced method.
- The more you shape, the more the dog offers behaviours, and the faster new things are learned.
- Shaping builds a thinking dog that keeps working through hard problems, plus mental stamina and body awareness.
- Trick ideas from the notes: back up, left/right spins, hand touch, weave between legs, sit up, paw touch, chin rest, take a nap, go to bed, play dead, roll over, lights on/off, open a drawer/door, put away toys, fetch keys/remote/blanket.
- Video: shaping a 12-week puppy to back onto an object: https://youtu.be/CpA25qUBxiA

**More:** [Shaping a 12-week puppy to back onto an object](https://youtu.be/CpA25qUBxiA)

<sub>Sources: F06, F07, F11, F12</sub>

<a id="shaping-game-humans"></a>
### The Shaping Game (practice with people)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation*

Practise clicker timing on a human 'dog' and feel how hard it is to learn without words.

**How to**
1. 2+ people. One is the 'dog' and leaves the room; the rest pick a behaviour (turn on a light, sit on the floor with hands on head…).
2. One trainer, one 'dog'. No words, gestures, sounds or eye hints.
3. Click for movement in the right direction/closer to the goal, like 'hot and cold'.

**Tips**
- Shows how a late click leads to the wrong behaviour.

<sub>Sources: F07</sub>

## Engagement & focus

<a id="cookie-fingers"></a>
### Cookie fingers
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation, personal*

A signal that means 'come to this hand, there's food'. The basis for luring and for many handling moves.

**How to**
1. Treat in hand, held at the dog's nose height.
2. Shake your wrist/hand up and down; the dog follows the hand.
3. Practise with both hands.

**Tips**
- Teaches the dog to watch your body language and value your hand.
- For handling, cookie fingers stay low, by your knee (F13).

**Leads to:** [Lap turn (cookie fingers)](#lap-turn), [Flip away (turning away from you)](#flip-away), [Target plate: value & sends](#send-to-target-plate)

<sub>Sources: F08, F11, F13, NOTES</sub>

<a id="hand-target"></a>
### Hand targeting ('Touch')
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation, personal · Cues: 'Touch', 'Target'*

Nose-to-palm touch. A portable focus tool, a distraction from trouble, and the first step toward contact training.

**How to**
1. Put a handful of small treats in one hand. In the other, hold one treat between two fingers of an open palm.
2. Present the open palm so the dog sees/smells the treat.
3. When the nose touches the palm, say 'Yes' as the dog eats the treat.
4. Wean the finger treat; present an empty palm and add 'Touch'. When the dog bumps it, reward by presenting the treat on the palm where they touched.

**Progressions**
- `PK2` Treat between fingers > empty palm with 'Touch'.
- `F12` Goal: firm 'bird-peck' touches at different heights and angles: low, away from your body, up on hind legs.
- `F14` Use a small plastic target held in the hand; add 'Touch'/'Target' once the dog touches reliably when it's offered.

**Tips**
- Great for redirecting a puppy or getting attention in stressful situations.
- Your notes say 'another dog can smell/see it'; that should probably read 'the dog'.

**Leads to:** [Contact training: 2on2off](#two-on-two-off)

<sub>Sources: PK2, PK3, PK4, F12, F14, NOTES</sub>

<a id="push-away-and-run"></a>
### Push Away and Run
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation*

Dogs want what they can't have; make that you.

**How to**
1. Gently push the dog away on the chest or side.
2. Run away and call.
3. When the dog catches up, reward with food, a toy, or lots of play.

<sub>Sources: F03, F04</sub>

<a id="find-scatter"></a>
### 'Find' / 'Scatter' (treats on the ground)
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation · Cues: 'Find', 'Search', 'Scatter'*

Permission to scavenge for a moment. Gets attention, removes attention from something else, and resets the dog's position.

**How to**
1. 'Find'/'Search' = throw one treat. 'Scatter' = throw 4 or more.
2. Example: release off a contact with 'Okay' then 'Find' (throw forward); next throw a treat behind the board so you can reload the target plate and the dog can re-load into 2on2off.

**Tips**
- Match treat value to how high the distractions are.

<sub>Sources: F18</sub>

## Self-control & impulse control

<a id="its-your-choice"></a>
### It's Your Choice (the 'Mugging' game)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation, personal · Also called: Mugging Game*

Self-control taught through body language only: the treats are available when the dog stops trying to take them.

**How to**
1. Hold several small treats on an open palm in front of the dog.
2. If the dog grabs, nuzzles, paws or chews: just close your hand into a fist. Don't pull away. Say NOTHING; no verbal correction.
3. Open again. When the dog doesn't go for the treats, mark 'Yes' and, with your other hand, take one treat off the palm and feed it.
4. Repeat, rewarding every right choice.

**Progressions**
- `F03` Move the open hand around: up, down, to the side.
- `F03` Sit on the floor beside the dog; put 1 treat on the floor by its paws. Cover it with your hand if the dog goes for it; when it stops, pick the treat up and feed with 'Yes'.
- `F04` Dog in a sit or down. Treat on the floor in front; then on 1 paw, 2 paws, multiple treats. Raise treat value.
- `F05` How many treats can you put on the paws/floor before 'Yes'? (class challenge)
- `F07` Use it for stays: dog in sit/down/stand, open hand, step sideways. If they break, close hand and reset; if they hold, return and reward with 'Yes'.
- `F12` How far can you go: laterally, in front, behind?

**Tips**
- Why 'mugging'? The dog 'mugs' your hand (grabbing, pawing, nuzzling) to rob you of the treats. The class's official name is 'It's Your Choice'.
- Timing matters: open/close the hand at the right moment by reading body language (PK3).
- The same idea runs through crate games, leave it, and door waits.

**Leads to:** [Sit & position stays](#sit-and-stays), [Leave it](#leave-it), [Take it / Get it / OK (permission) & 'This and That'](#take-it)

<sub>Sources: F02, F03, F04, F05, F07, F08, F12, PK1, PK3, PK4, NOTES</sub>

<a id="leave-it"></a>
### Leave it
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation, personal · Cues: 'Leave It'*

Don't touch that: food on the floor, a sock, a toy. A safety skill (e.g. broken glass with food on the kitchen floor), and later used in agility when a toy on the ground is the reward.

**Before this:** [It's Your Choice (the 'Mugging' game)](#its-your-choice)

**How to**
1. Class method (F08): lead the dog to a food container (or toy) to sniff, say 'Leave It' and redirect, then feed a treat from you. Repeat a few times.
2. Then play 'This and That' (see Take it): 'Ready'... 'Get It' gives permission.
3. Your method A: drop a kibble far enough away that the dog can't easily get it, say 'leave it', reward with a better treat from you. Slowly decrease the distance.
4. Your method B (toys): on leash, walk past a good toy (or treat), say 'leave it' and keep walking with gentle leash pressure. Start further away if needed.

**Progressions**
- `F08` Increase temptation: open the food container.
- `NOTES` Test: throw a good treat nearby, say 'leave it', and the dog comes to you instead.
- `NOTES` Test: off leash, walking close by the toy.

**Leads to:** [Take it / Get it / OK (permission) & 'This and That'](#take-it)

<sub>Sources: F08, NOTES</sub>

<a id="take-it"></a>
### Take it / Get it / OK (permission) & 'This and That'
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation, personal · Cues: 'Get It', 'Take It', 'OK', 'Ready'*

The dog waits for permission instead of lunging at dropped things. Builds patience and impulse control.

**How to**
1. Drop a kibble and cover it with your hand. Let the dog sniff, but don't lift your hand until they back off.
2. Then say 'take it' (or 'get it'/'ok') and let them eat it.
3. Slowly lengthen the wait before the release.
4. Do the same with toys.
5. 'This and That' (F08): alternate 'Leave It' with 'Ready'... 'Get It' so the dog learns the difference between not allowed and allowed.

**Tips**
- Class meaning: 'Ready' = get ready, look ahead/focus; 'Get It' = permission to go do it.

**Leads to:** [Collar grab](#collar-grab), [Target plate: value & sends](#send-to-target-plate)

<sub>Sources: F04, F06, F08, NOTES</sub>

<a id="crate-games"></a>
### Crate games ('Your In, Your Out')
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation · Cues: 'Kennel', 'Crate', 'Bed', 'In'*

Make the crate a happy, safe place to drive into and wait in. Useful for class, trials, travel, and visiting.

**You'll need:** crate (take the pad out at first so treats are easy to find); treats; tug toy

**How to**
1. Throw a couple of treats to the back of the crate.
2. As soon as they're eaten, release the dog by throwing a treat outside the crate (or reward coming out with a quick tug).

**Progressions**
- `F03` Build speed in and out. Once the dog drives in fast, add the verbal cue.
- `F03` Make the dog wait a minute inside before releasing.
- `F03` Close the door for a minute, then open and release. If the dog tries to leave before release, the door closes. No 'No'; it's another It's Your Choice game.
- `F08` Do it without words, and with gates and doors as well as crates.

**Troubleshooting**
- Nervous or crate-shy dog: feed every meal at the back of the crate with the door open and let them choose to go in (F02).

**Tips**
- Video: https://youtu.be/FQ7evQmSWYk

**Leads to:** [Waiting at doors](#door-waits)

**More:** [Starting dogs to value their crate](https://youtu.be/FQ7evQmSWYk)

<sub>Sources: F02, F03, F08</sub>

<a id="door-waits"></a>
### Waiting at doors
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: personal, foundation*

Safety skill: the dog never rushes through an opening door, whichever side you're on.

**Before this:** [Crate games ('Your In, Your Out')](#crate-games), [It's Your Choice (the 'Mugging' game)](#its-your-choice)

**How to**
1. Practise with the dog in their crate or at any door.
2. Use the It's Your Choice / crate-game logic (F03, F08): the door opens only while the dog holds position; if the dog moves toward the gap, the door closes, with no words.
3. Release with your release word, and reward.

> ⚠️ Your notes say 'Description…'. The steps above are taken from the class's crate-door progression (F03) and 'Your In Your Out with crates, gates, doors' (F08). The class notes don't have a separate door lesson.

<sub>Sources: NOTES, F03, F08</sub>

## Puppy life skills, handling & calm

<a id="puppy-management"></a>
### Puppy management at home
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy · Cues: 'Hurry Up'*

Set the puppy up to make good choices outside the crate.

**How to**
1. House leash on whenever the puppy is out, so you can calmly guide/redirect.
2. Supervise constantly; limit space (e.g. one room with a baby gate).
3. Manage schedule and behaviours as they come up.
4. Do many tiny training sessions through the day, e.g. potty breaks with a 'Hurry Up' cue; praise/pet/treat when they go.

<sub>Sources: PK1</sub>

<a id="play-and-settle"></a>
### Play & Settle
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy · Cues: 'Settle', 'Good', 'Yes'*

Teach a puppy to switch from excited play to calm, and to play with you appropriately (bite control).

**How to**
1. Play on your knees (taller = safer from face-biting). Gently push the pup away, wiggle fingers, provoke play.
2. Before the pup gets too wound up: hand in the collar (and hold the leash so they can't grab it), say 'Settle'.
3. When they relax, release collar pressure, say 'Yes', feed, and keep praising quietly ('Good boy, nice').
4. Restart play, then settle again before it escalates. Short spurts.

**Progressions**
- `PK3` Use it whenever the pup gets over-excited and nips.
- `PK4` Add more distractions; use during Meet & Greet.

**Tips**
- No food in your hand while playing; it turns play into wanting the food.
- If the pup grabs the leash, take it away and redirect to play with you.

**Leads to:** [Meet & greet a person](#meet-and-greet)

<a id="collar-grab"></a>
### Collar grab
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation · Cues: 'Ready', 'Get It', 'Settle'*

Make being caught rewarding, so the dog doesn't dodge when you reach for it. It's also the start of the drive/focus games.

**How to**
1. Touch/hold the collar, then reward.
2. Say the dog's name, hold the collar with a bit of pressure, feed, release the collar.

**Progressions**
- `F02` Touch/hold collar > treat.
- `F03` Name > hold with light pressure > feed > release.
- `F04` Collar Grab to Hand: hold collar, treat in your other hand ~2 ft from the face. 'Ready'... 'Get It' and release to the hand.
- `F06` Collar Grab to Target Plate (see Target plate).
- `PK3` Puppy husbandry: collar grab plus handling ears, teeth, feet. If they wiggle, keep holding and say 'Settle'; reward when they relax. Best when pup is a bit tired.
- `PK4` Hold front and back feet, spread toes as if trimming nails; then introduce the clippers (touch, clipping sound).

**Leads to:** [Target plate: value & sends](#send-to-target-plate), [Collar & body hold](#collar-body-hold)

<sub>Sources: F02, F03, F04, F06, PK3, PK4</sub>

<a id="collar-body-hold"></a>
### Collar & body hold
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy*

A secure restraint hold for vet exams and handling; also helps with puppy biting.

**How to**
1. One hand runs over the back and under the tummy; the other under the neck and up toward the ear.
2. Hold the pup securely against your body for just a moment, talking calmly ('Good puppy, well done').
3. If calm and relaxed: put down, 'Yes', reward. Don't wait for wiggling; if they wiggle, try again and release sooner after they go still.
4. Optional: a helper rewards while you hold.
5. Gradually lengthen the hold. Goal: a secure, relaxed 'limp noodle'.

<sub>Sources: PK2, PK3</sub>

<a id="meet-and-greet"></a>
### Meet & greet a person
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: puppy · Cues: 'Settle'*

Stay calm when a new person approaches.

**Before this:** [Play & Settle](#play-and-settle), [Collar grab](#collar-grab)

**How to**
1. Step 1: Walk the pup toward a seated person who ignores it. At any lunge/jump, collar grab and 'Settle'; when calm, release pressure, 'Good puppy', treat.
2. Step 2: Pup in sit/stand/down; a person (no dog) walks toward you. If the pup gets excited, hand in collar and 'Settle'; reward calm.
3. If the pup won't settle: step on the leash and let the pup self-correct, no words; praise and treat the moment they settle.
4. Visitor takes another step with each success, until they can stand and chat. Later the visitor may offer a treat if the pup stays calm.

<sub>Sources: PK4</sub>

## Name & recall

<a id="name-game"></a>
### Name Game (response to name)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation, personal*

The dog snaps its head to you when it hears its name. This is the first 'recall' a puppy learns.

**How to**
1. Pre-load 5–8 treats in your hand. Sitting/standing doesn't matter; this isn't obedience.
2. Wait until the dog is NOT looking at you, then say its name once.
3. The instant the head flicks toward you, feed.
4. Repeat & treat.

**Progressions**
- `F02` No distractions > gradually add distractions, close and at distance.
- `F03` Aim for an instant head flick; jackpot (3–4 treats) a great response.
- `PK3` Add motion: say name and back up; let the pup catch up, then feed.

**Tips**
- Name = 'head check' (look at me and wait for the next instruction), not 'come here'. In agility the name is a redirect (F03).
- Have a throwaway cue too (see Informal recall cue) so the name never gets 'spoiled'.
- Great for waiting your turn in class.

**Leads to:** [Formal recall: 'Here' / 'Come'](#recall-here-come), [Informal 'throwaway' cue](#informal-recall-cue)

<a id="informal-recall-cue"></a>
### Informal 'throwaway' cue
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation · Cues: 'pup pup pup', 'kissy noise'*

Protect the value of the name and the formal recall by having a casual word for when success isn't likely or you aren't prepared to reward.

**How to**
1. Pick a short fun sound, e.g. high-pitched 'pup pup pup' or a kissing noise.
2. Use it to engage lightly when the dog may not respond to its name.

<a id="recall-here-come"></a>
### Formal recall: 'Here' / 'Come'
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation, puppy, personal · Cues: 'Here', 'Come'*

Emergency-level cue: come right to me, fast. Possibly lifesaving.

**Before this:** [Name Game (response to name)](#name-game)

**How to**
1. Use ONLY with great rewards.
2. With multiple dogs: name first (gets that dog's head check), then 'Here'.
3. Train it with restrained recalls (see next).

> ⚠️ Your notes say 'Describe…'. Steps above are taken from F03/F05/PK4.

**Leads to:** [Restrained recall](#restrained-recall)

<a id="restrained-recall"></a>
### Restrained recall
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation, puppy · Cues: '(name)', 'Here', 'Come'*

Build a fast, excited recall to a specific side.

**You'll need:** helper to hold the dog; very visual favourite toy (or food); long line if needed

**How to**
1. Helper holds the dog. Step out about 30 ft (food-motivated dogs: show food first).
2. Without turning your body, look over your right shoulder (show your cheek), say the name, then immediately 'Here'/'Come'.
3. As you call, drop your right hand by your side and show the toy, so the dog comes to your right side.
4. On arrival: tug party (or jackpot several treats, excited voice).
5. Practise both sides.

**Progressions**
- `PK4` Puppy version: hold pup, handler runs away. Run > call name > 'Here'/'Come'. Look over your shoulder to the side you want.
- `F06` Restrained recall into a blind cross (see Blind cross on the flat).

**Leads to:** [Blind cross on the flat (with restrained recall)](#blind-cross-flat)

## Positions, mat & leash

<a id="sit-and-stays"></a>
### Sit & position stays
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation, puppy · Cues: 'Sit'*

Respond to the first cue and hold the position until released.

**Before this:** [It's Your Choice (the 'Mugging' game)](#its-your-choice)

**How to**
1. Ask 'Sit' once at your side; if needed, lure into position with no extra hand signals.
2. Don't say 'Stay'/'Wait': the position itself means hold it until released.
3. Reward often while the dog is in position, then slowly build duration.
4. Add distance using the It's Your Choice open hand (see its progressions).

**Progressions**
- `PK4` Puppy: sit, open hand with treats nearest the pup, one small sideways step. Break = close hand and reset; hold = step back, feed, 'Yes, Good Puppy'.
- `F12` See how far you can go: sideways, in front, behind.

<sub>Sources: F07, F08, F12, PK4</sub>

<a id="mat-work"></a>
### Mat work ('Go to bed' / 'Place')
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, personal · Cues: 'Go on your Bed', 'Go to your Mat', 'OK' · Also called: Place, Go to Bed*

Going to the mat means lie down and relax until released.

**How to**
1. Lure onto the bed/mat while saying 'Go on your bed'. 'Yes' and treat when on it; 'Good' while they stay.
2. Release BEFORE the pup leaves: show a treat, throw it 2–3 ft away, say 'OK'.

**Progressions**
- `PK3` Lure into a down on the mat (don't cue 'down'); the mat should come to mean 'lie down'. Feed in position, then hold collar, throw a treat, release quickly before they break.
- `PK4` Build time in a down; give a Kong/bone to settle for a few minutes (supervise). Always release from the down with a thrown treat and 'OK'.
- `NOTES` Test commitment with 'Boundary Games' (free PDF from Absolute Dogs).

**Tips**

### Off
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: personal · Cues: 'Off'*

Get off the bed, chair, or whatever.

**How to**
1. Lure the dog onto something (not too high).
2. Say 'off' and throw a kibble/treat on the floor.

<a id="loose-leash-walking"></a>
### Loose leash walking
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy*

Walk with a loose 'J' in the leash.

**You'll need:** leash; treats/treat pouch

**How to**
1. Start somewhere quiet (a hallway is ideal).
2. Pup on one side; leash in the opposite hand so it hangs in a 'J' from the collar. Treats in the hand nearest the pup (or a pouch on that hip).
3. Walk briskly, like you're late.
4. The moment the pup passes your hip, turn 180°, encouraging with 'pup pup pup'.
5. When they follow, 'Yes' and treat.
6. At first you may get only a couple of steps before turning; gradually go further.

**Progressions**
- `PK4` Once successful, move to more distracting places (e.g. back yard).

## Toy play & motivation

<a id="tug-and-out"></a>
### Tug & 'Out' (toy exchange)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation · Cues: 'Out', 'Drop It', 'Give', 'Release', 'Mine'*

Teach the dog to play with you, tug, and let go on cue.

**You'll need:** 2 toys the dog loves (two identical ones if the pup prefers one); lead or long line

**How to**
1. Play on your knees (discourages face-biting). Wiggle the toy on the ground; push the pup away a little to make it more exciting.
2. Tug a few times, then stop and present the second toy while saying 'Out'. Hide the first toy behind your back if needed.
3. Repeat for a minute or two; stop before the pup tires or loses interest.
4. If teeth hit hand/clothes: hand in collar to stop (a toneless 'Ouch' or a Play & Settle), then restart. Biting ends the game.

**Progressions**
- `F08` Adult dogs: let the dog bring the toy, tug a bit first, then 'Out' and trade for high-value food or another toy.
- `F08` Control the game: dog/toy on a lead or long line, or train in a hallway. Don't let the dog self-reward by running off with the toy.
- `F08` Next: 'Put It in My Hand'. The toy is delivered to your hand, not dropped a few feet away.

<a id="build-toy-drive"></a>
### Building toy drive (Susan Garrett method)
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation*

Create a dog that loves a special toy, for rewarding at a distance (sends, weaves, gambles).

**You'll need:** throwable toy that doesn't roll much (tug rope, ball in a sock, stuffie); ~3 m light line attached; a special drawer to keep it in

**How to**
1. Keep the toy in a drawer in your main living area. The dog only sees it during this routine (and later at class).
2. Before meals, act goofy and say silly things on your way to the drawer; slowly open it.
3. Pause, then whip the toy out like you found $50 and run with it into the next room.
4. Swing and drag it on the line like a fishing lure, having a great time, but don't let the dog get it at first.
5. After 1–2 minutes, rush it back to the drawer ('oh no, it's gone') and carry on as normal.
6. Repeat 2–3 times a day. After day 2, allow a few seconds' grab, playfully steal it back via the line, play alone a bit, put it away.
7. Gradually lengthen real tug games with you.

**Tips**
- Food-mad, toy-indifferent dogs: simmer the toy in liver or chicken broth, but NEVER leave the dog alone with it (risk of swallowing/surgery).
- Be sincerely having fun or it won't work. No cues for anything during the routine.
- Remove other toys while doing this; leave only chews.
- Works especially well with puppies.

**More:** [Play like a dog](https://eg227.keap-link019.com/v2/click/80d6bf7dac154a2271575276e22bdef7/eJyNkEFrwzAMhf-LzvFch9BkuZVSSkjXw9jOQ0u0zNSxjaMUTMl_nztGTxvsqvfeJz3dBKMDx3UnjEBbFKVYiIAteULHu9ExtN-i1nq9KqqF6Ml9HsKYvDC338IP_T4tq1LnDE8es-Xlebtr6vPh7Vifm2z1EPKS_3A2uirL1fpB2p-29VHM859oHIj310yPwnBIeC_VUS7Gr6HP_guzj0apmCI4CyEgczdasNQTT0_tOKhiqTdqWSlLw0AoO2DJyUq-oBwTy4A9QkRJUX6kyDKnJQcgR86qfAF4j677-WCDkzDv0EecvwAgkXm3)

## Body awareness & confidence

<a id="novel-surfaces"></a>
### Luring onto objects & new surfaces
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: puppy, foundation, personal · Cues: 'Feet', 'Good', 'Yes' · Also called: Standing on weird things*

Confidence on different textures and objects; the foundation for perch work and contacts.

**You'll need:** cardboard, contact board, exercise mat/platform, upside-down dog bowl, squishy balance mat

**How to**
1. Lure the dog across the surface; give lots of treats just for trying.
2. Then lure front feet onto it ('feet'), and later back feet only.
3. Increase time on the object: 'Good' = keep going, 'Yes' + reward = done.

**Progressions**
- `F03` Lure onto more objects; longer duration.
- `PK3` Practise positions (sit, down, stand) on the surface, luring if needed and adding the word.

**Tips**
- Make sure the surface is safe for their feet.
- Strength/balance exercises on unstable surfaces: get professional advice.

**Leads to:** [Perch work: rear-end awareness (pivots)](#perch-work), ['Feet' (front feet on a target)](#feet)

<a id="perch-work"></a>
### Perch work: rear-end awareness (pivots)
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation, personal · Also called: Rear end awareness, Hind end awareness, Pivot, Moving off pressure*

Teach the dog it has a back end it can move independently while the front feet stay put, and to move away from handler pressure.

**You'll need:** sturdy perch: upside-down bowl, washing tub, perch; treats or a lickable (squeeze tube of soft food/peanut butter)

**Before this:** [Luring onto objects & new surfaces](#novel-surfaces)

**How to**
1. Prerequisite: dog happily stands on novel objects for a few seconds.
2. Lure (or shape with the clicker, F06) the front two feet onto the perch.
3. Stand beside the dog, treat at its nose, and step into the dog ('putting pressure on').
4. When the hind end moves away while front feet stay on: 'Yes'/click and reward.
5. Start with one step; build to a full circle.
6. Switch hands/sides and go the other way.

**Tips**
- The word you were looking for: 'perch work' or 'pivots', a.k.a. hind-end / rear-end awareness. The class also calls it 'moving off pressure'.
- Both directions matter: builds rear-end awareness, muscle tone and balance on both sides.

**Leads to:** [Contact training: 2on2off](#two-on-two-off)

**More:** [Strengthening skills for agility](https://hubs.li/Q03DhCh40)

<sub>Sources: F04, F05, F06, NOTES</sub>

<a id="two-on-two-off"></a>
### Contact training: 2on2off
*Level 3 (Advanced) · Programs: foundation · Cues: 'OK', 'Break', 'Alright' · Also called: 2o2o*

Stop at the bottom of a contact (dogwalk, teeter, A-frame): back feet in the yellow zone, front feet on the ground, until released.

**You'll need:** contact plank / box; treats; target plate

**Before this:** [Hand targeting ('Touch')](#hand-target), [Perch work: rear-end awareness (pivots)](#perch-work)

**How to**
1. Sit on the ground in front of (later beside) the plank.
2. Lure the dog onto the plank with a treat at its nose.
3. Slowly bring your hand to the ground close to the plank so the front paws step down.
4. Praise and place a treat between the front paws; keep feeding there, with a new treat ready before the mouth is empty.
5. While the dog eats, move your hand away from the nose and back.
6. If the dog leaves, stop talking and lure back into position.
7. After a few treats, when the dog looks at you, say the release word and drop/throw a treat away from the plank.

**Tips**
- Introduce the release word from day one.
- Find the distance from the plank where the position is easy to hold.
- Hand targeting is the beginning of contact work (F12).

## Agility flatwork & handling

<a id="flatwork-overview"></a>
### What flatwork is & why it matters
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation*

Flatwork is everything between the obstacles: where you set up each approach and do most of your handling.

**How to**
1. Teach handling maneuvers without obstacles first, so your body cues become clear, consistent, coherent and timely.
2. Foundation flatwork includes restrained recalls, circle work, handling cues with one jump, and target plate work.

<sub>Sources: F04, F06</sub>

<a id="circle-work"></a>
### Circle work (shadow handling)
*Level 1 (Start here) · Programs: foundation · Also called: Shadow handling, Flatwork*

Build value for the dog's position at your side (the 'reinforcement zone') at any speed.

**You'll need:** flat collar only; 6 ft leash; small food rewards (no tug yet); an object to circle: chair, tree, barrel, pylon; spatula with peanut butter etc. for small dogs

**How to**
1. Walk a circle around an object with the dog on the OUTSIDE. Clockwise = dog on your left.
2. Loose leash in the right hand; treats in the hand nearest the dog (tie the leash around your waist if feeding is awkward).
3. Feed constantly for correct position. High rate of reinforcement.
4. After 5–6 treats, break off and play (little tug games are good stress breaks).
5. Work both directions. No verbal corrections.

**Progressions**
- `F02` Walk, dog on the outside, lots of food.
- `F03` Add speed on the outside; add straight-line parallel work.
- `F04` Inside circles at a walk (more pressure; the dog may cut in front or behind). Be patient, break with play, then add speed.
- `F05` Outside + inside on a loose leash; build speed when ready.

**What 'right' looks like**
- Dog stays at your side, body aligned forward, through acceleration and deceleration.
- No forging, lagging, bouncing, pushing your line, or shaping the circle.
- No crossing in front (flanking), behind (blind crossing), spinning, or flicking away. Watch especially for swinging wide when you slow down.

**Tips**
- Be consistent all day: don't let the dog practise crossing your path or flicking away even when walking to/from the car or kennel (F03).
- Small dogs: stay upright; don't bend over to feed.
- Video: Shape Up Agility circle work https://youtu.be/6OHVdhE9DqY

**More:** [Circle work](https://youtu.be/6OHVdhE9DqY)

<sub>Sources: F02, F03, F04, F05</sub>

<a id="send-to-target-plate"></a>
### Target plate: value & sends
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation · Cues: 'Ready', 'Get It', 'OK', 'Break', 'Alright'*

Teach the dog to look and drive ahead, independently of the handler, so you're free to handle.

**You'll need:** target plate; treats

**Before this:** [Collar grab](#collar-grab), [Take it / Get it / OK (permission) & 'This and That'](#take-it)

**How to**
1. Load the plate with food 2–3 ft away.
2. Collar grab, say 'Ready' (= look ahead).
3. When the dog flips its head to the plate, release with 'Get It' and race the dog to the plate.
4. Move the plate further away as drive and speed grow.

**Progressions**
- `F06` Collar grab > Ready > head flips to plate > Get It.
- `F10` Send with handler motion: lead out ~6 ft, look over your shoulder, dog-side arm back; release ('Get It'/'OK'), swing the arm forward to send. As the dog passes you, move forward with it. Goal: the dog runs PAST you while you're moving.
- `F11` 45° angle: lead out ~10 ft at 45° to the dog and plate. Look over your shoulder, upper body turned, dog-side leg and arm back, shake cookie fingers. Release 'OK', draw the dog around that side, then send to the plate.
- `F12` Practise straight, 45° and 90° sends.
- `F14` Review handling to the plate at 45° and 90°.

**More:** [What is a Backside Send in dog agility, and how do you teach it?](https://www.oneminddogs.com/blog/what-is-a-backside-send-in-dog-agility-and-how-do-you-teach-it/)

<a id="blind-cross-flat"></a>
### Blind cross on the flat (with restrained recall)
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation*

Handler changes sides in front of the dog; the dog switches sides behind the handler's back.

**Before this:** [Restrained recall](#restrained-recall)

**How to**
1. A helper holds the dog while you move forward.
2. Treat or toy in each hand, hands pointing down and slightly out at hip height.
3. Look to the side you want the dog on ('power of the cheek') and call its name. That arm guides until the dog commits to it.
4. After commitment, turn your head to the opposite side where you want the dog next.
5. Reward the dog at the hand that's out.

<a id="lap-turn"></a>
### Lap turn (cookie fingers)
*Level 2 (Building) · Programs: foundation*

Turn the dog 180° away from you. Dogs naturally turn toward the handler, so this builds body awareness, two-sidedness, lead changes and following handling.

**Before this:** [Cookie fingers](#cookie-fingers), [Flip away (turning away from you)](#flip-away)

**How to**
1. Treat at the dog's nose height. Back up shaking cookie fingers so the dog follows your hand.
2. As the dog reaches you, keep drawing it past your hip, then turn it away by rotating your wrist (left hand = clockwise, right hand = counter-clockwise).
3. Once it commits to the turn, you both move forward, parallel. Feed.
4. Start slow; add speed once smooth. Practise both sides.

**Progressions**
- `F08` On the flat: back up, turn 180°, move forward and feed.
- `F10` When turning is easy, throw a ball/toy forward so the dog powers out.
- `F11` Add speed; throw the toy forward and keep moving parallel; tug at the toy.
- `F12` Smooth the turn, all 4 feet on the ground; try to beat the dog to the toy.
- `F13` Around a cone/wing: dog on your left, face the dog, cookie fingers low by the left knee; back toward the cone. Turn the dog away (clockwise) around the cone. Your dog-side (left) leg and arm go BACK while you wait for commitment; feed if the dog struggles to commit. Then move forward parallel and throw the toy (right hand) on the dog's path. Repeat on the right side.
- `F14` Around an obstacle: start facing each other, guiding arm low, wait for commitment, reward tight turns. Then send from further away. Goal: 2–15 ft without going to the obstacle's plane, while staying connected.

**Tips**
- Diagram: Week 13 lap turn steps 1–3 (lap_turn_week13_diagram.gif). Video: Week 13 Lap Turn.

**Leads to:** [Wrap (180° around an obstacle from the front)](#wrap)

### Wrap (180° around an obstacle from the front)
*Level 3 (Advanced) · Programs: foundation*

The dog drives forward to an obstacle and wraps tightly back around it.

<a id="connection-and-guiding-hand"></a>
### Staying connected & rewarding from the non-guiding hand
*Level 3 (Advanced) · Programs: foundation*

Balance handler focus with obstacle commitment so you can stay ahead of your dog.

**How to**
1. Look at the dog's path; your eyes draw theirs forward.
2. Stay connected ('power of the cheek'). You don't have to physically see the dog.
3. No sunglasses when working the dog.
4. From Week 18: stop holding treats in the guiding (dog-side) hand. Reward from the opposite hand, a target plate/food bowl, or a toy.

**Tips**
- A purely handler-focused dog must be escorted to every obstacle, which leaves you behind and pushing from the back. Value for going ahead and committing lets you get to the next obstacle first.

### Between
*Level 3 (Advanced) · Programs: personal · Cues: 'Between'*

Go between my legs and sit. A safe spot for small dogs where you can protect them.

**How to**
1. Treats in both hands.
2. Say 'between' and lure the dog toward you, but keep the lure hand going past and behind your leg.
3. Show the other hand to bring the dog forward through your legs; ask for the sit.

**More:** [5 Top Tricks Every Dog Should Know](https://absolute-dogs.com/blogs/gamechangers/5-top-tricks-every-dog-should-know-training-for-fun-and-practical-real-life-skills)
